How to study with a lecture recording

A recording only helps if you come back to it with a method. A PDF of the same class — transcript, slides and, when possible, a summary — makes review shorter than listening to 50 minutes again.

Updated 25 November 2026

Record to review, not to skip class

ResumAI organizes what was said. It does not attend class for you. The best use is this: you pay attention, capture the audio and the photos, and the same day you turn that into study material. Leaving a file untouched until the night before the exam usually fails — just like a notebook you never reopen.

A flow that fits a school day

  1. One record per lecture, with the course name and the date in the title, if the app lets you label it that way, or in a note at the start.
  2. During class, photograph what audio captures poorly: a diagram on the board, a formula, the reading list.
  3. When class ends, tap Finish. If the device has Apple Intelligence, the PDF includes the core idea, topics, insights and quotes, plus the transcript and photos. Without it, you still get the transcript and slides.
  4. Reread the PDF in ten minutes, still on campus or on the bus. Fix a name, circle what is likely to be on the exam, jot a question for the next class.
  5. In the weekly review, do not start with the audio. Start with the PDF. Go back to the recording only when a point is unclear.

On iPad, mark up the summary

ResumAI 1.2 adds Apple Pencil support on iPad: you can draw and annotate right on the summary PDF, and your marks are included in the exported PDF. You can underline a definition, link a topic to a problem-set question, or write “review” next to an uncertain passage — and keep those marks when you export the file.

Honest limits

The transcript will mishandle technical terms. The summary may omit a detail that, for your exam, is the detail. Cross-check with the instructor’s material. And again, record only if the class allows it. Publishing the audio or the PDF is usually a different matter.
