How to take notes at conferences and events

Conferences overwhelm memory. The useful habit is to treat each session as its own record and leave it with a PDF before you walk into the next one.

Updated 25 November 2026

One session, one record

If you leave the recorder running all day, the summary blends the keynote, the coffee break and the talk in the next room. In ResumAI, start a new record for every session. Finish when the applause ends, even if the next talk starts in ten minutes.

Name the record in a way you will recognize later: “Tuesday 10 a.m., room 2, speaker’s name.” By the end of the day, the list on your device is the program you actually attended.

What to capture besides audio

  • Photos of the slides you will not remember: a chart, a definition, an architecture diagram, the research agenda.
  • A short note when someone in the audience asks a good question, or when you disagree. The transcript captures speech; it does not capture your reaction.
  • The name of someone you want to look up later. Write it down immediately. Breaks erase that kind of detail.

The break is part of the method

Between sessions, tap Finish and let the PDF generate while you walk to the next room or get coffee. Skim the summary for a minute: if a proper name came out wrong, fix it now, not on the flight home.

If Apple Intelligence is not available on that device, you still leave with a transcript and slides. That is not the full brief, but it is better than a loose 50-minute audio file.

Crowded rooms, difficult microphones

In a large hall, sit closer to the stage or near a loudspeaker. The iPhone microphone hears the room: chatter in the row behind you ends up in the recording and hurts transcription. A headset mic pointed at the stage can help, if you have one — and if the event allows recording.

As with any talk, follow the conference rules. Many events allow personal notes and forbid publishing the audio or the PDF. The fact that your content stays on the device, or goes only to Apple Private Cloud Compute, does not give you permission to share what was said.

At the end of the day

The PDFs stay on the device, in the same app. You decide what to export or share. There is no account of ours holding the conference history. If you travel the next day, export the summaries that matter to wherever you already keep files — and nothing more.

Apple Pencil on iPad (version 1.2)

On iPad, ResumAI 1.2 adds Apple Pencil support: you can draw and annotate right on the summary PDF, and your marks are included in the exported PDF. That comes after the PDF is generated — during the talk, the essentials are still the audio, the slide photos and a short note. There is a dedicated guide: Apple Pencil notes at a talk.
